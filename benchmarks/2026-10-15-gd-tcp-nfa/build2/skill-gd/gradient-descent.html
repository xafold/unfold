Why the learning rate decides

For this loss, each step multiplies the gap by 1 − 2η. So η = 0.5 lands on 3 in one step, and any η above 1 makes the gap grow.

For a real loss, nobody knows the best η in advance, so people try several values.

What the figure leaves out

Left outWhat really happens
Many weightsA model has millions of weights. The gradient holds one slope per weight, and each weight takes its own step.
Curved lossesA real loss has hills, flat areas and more than one low point. The slope changes at a different rate in each place.
Mini-batchesStochastic gradient descent (SGD) computes the slope on a small sample of the data. Each step is then a little noisy.
Changing ηA schedule makes η smaller during training. Momentum and Adam also change the step size for each weight.
When to stopTraining stops after a fixed number of steps, or when the loss stops falling.
