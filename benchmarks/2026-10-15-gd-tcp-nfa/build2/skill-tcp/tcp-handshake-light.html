What uses a sequence number

An ack is one more than the last number it covers. That is why the ack after a SYN or a FIN is seq + 1.

Terms

ISNinitial sequence number, picked by each side at SYN
seqnumber of the first byte (or SYN, FIN) in a segment
acknext number the sender of the ack expects
SND.NXTnext sequence number this side will send
SND.UNAoldest number this side sent that is not acknowledged
RCV.NXTnext sequence number this side expects to receive

half-closeone direction closed, the other still open

What the figure leaves out

topicwhat really happens
random ISNLinux hashes the addresses with a secret and adds a clock (RFC 6528).
relative numbersWireshark shows seq − ISN by default, so each SYN shows as 0.
wraparoundSequence numbers are 32-bit and wrap modulo 2³².
three-segment closeA server that closes at once sends one FIN,ACK that also acknowledges the client's FIN.
delayed ACKA receiver may wait 40 to 200 ms (Linux) to combine ACKs.
window and optionsEvery segment also carries a window size. SYNs carry MSS, window scale, SACK and timestamps.
loss and RSTLost segments are sent again after a timeout. RST aborts a connection without FIN.
TIME-WAIT lengthLinux waits a fixed 60 s, not a measured 2·MSL.
