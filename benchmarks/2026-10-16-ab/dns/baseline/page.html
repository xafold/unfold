How a DNS lookup for www.example.com finds its answer

Your laptop asks one recursive resolver. The resolver then walks down the DNS tree: the root, the .com TLD, and the authoritative server for example.com. Step through the first lookup. Then switch to a second lookup and drag the clock to see what the cache saves.

  • The laptop sends a recursive query (flag rd: "please find the full answer for me"). The resolver sends iterative queries: each server answers with what it knows, or with a referral one level down.

  • TTL (time to live) is the number of seconds a record may stay in a cache. The values here are typical: 2 days for the .com delegation, 1 day for the example.com delegation, 1 hour for the address. Round-trip times are example values.

  • Real root and TLD referrals list 13 name servers each. This page shows one of them. Your browser and operating system also keep small caches of their own, in front of the resolver.
